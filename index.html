Bioscience Case Study

Optimizing MARDI MR 297 Growth Rates

In Malaysia, securing domestic rice supply is a key pillar of National Food Security. Under the National Agrofood Policy 2.0 (2021-2030), Malaysia aims to increase its Self-Sufficiency Level (SSL) for rice to 75-80%.

The Problem: To maximize the harvest yield of the high-performance MARDI MR 297 paddy variety (110–120 day maturation cycle), nitrogen fertilizer applications must be synchronized exactly with the plant's peak vegetative growth rate. Applying fertilizer too early leads to high nutrient runoff into local waterways; applying it too late results in stunted panicle development.

Why Calculus? Cumulative dry biomass $f(t)$ follows an S-shaped sigmoidal growth curve. The **First Derivative $f'(t)$** represents the instantaneous growth rate (g/day). By finding the maximum value of $f'(t)$ (where the second derivative is zero, $f''(t) = 0$), agronomists pinpoint the precise date of maximum nutrient demand.

Mathematical Modeling

The Growth Functions

Cumulative Dry Biomass S-Curve
$$f(t) = \frac{K}{1 + A \cdot e^{-r \cdot t}}$$
$$f(t) = \frac{60}{1 + 29 \cdot e^{-0.07 \cdot t}}$$
K (Max Biomass): 60g / hill
A (Seedling Constant): 29
r (Growth Factor): 0.07/day
Daily Growth Rate (Derivative) Bell Curve
$$f'(t) = \frac{K \cdot r \cdot A \cdot e^{-r \cdot t}}{(1 + A \cdot e^{-r \cdot t})^2}$$
$$f'(t) = \frac{121.8 \cdot e^{-0.07 \cdot t}}{(1 + 29 \cdot e^{-0.07 \cdot t})^2}$$

Equivalent to the differential rate equation: $f'(t) = r \cdot f(t) \cdot \left(1 - \frac{f(t)}{K}\right)$
